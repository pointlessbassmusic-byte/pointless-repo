# Is passive quoting profitable? No.

_2026-09-23_

Spread capture was the last hypothesis standing: it is structural rather
than predictive, so it does not require out-forecasting the line that
every model in this repo loses to. It does not pay either.

## Correction: the first version of this note used a contaminated sample

The first version scored 500,170 trades it called "pre-match" — every
trade before `occurrence_datetime`. That timestamp is not the start.
`occurrence_datetime == expected_expiration_time` on every market checked;
it is the expected **settle** bound, and on tennis the tape shows live
price action a median **2.7 hours before it** (90% of markets). 90.7% of
those 500k trades sat within 3h of it and were in-play. The dramatic
by-price pattern the first version reported — prices diverging from 0.50
by 10–19¢ over 30 minutes — was matches resolving, not a maker edge, and
it vanishes completely once in-play is removed.

Nothing below relies on Kalshi's timestamp. Each market's pre-match window
is defined from its own tape: everything before the first print that moves
more than 10¢ from the opening price (`markout.prematch_cut`). A pre-match
line does not do that; a live one does within minutes.

## Sample

105 settled Kalshi tennis markets with a tape-defined pre-match window,
34,454 maker fills. Kalshi's tape says which resting order was filled:
`taker_book_side == "ask"` means the taker sold YES into a resting bid, so
the maker bought. Fills are marked to a later **trade** price rather than
a mid — the maker is credited only at a price someone actually dealt at.

## The flow is 84/16

| | fills | share |
|---|---|---|
| maker SOLD yes (taker bought) | 28,902 | **0.839** |
| maker BOUGHT yes (taker sold) | 5,552 | 0.161 |

A maker quoting both sides is hit **5× more on one side**. That is not
spread capture with a flat book — it is accumulating a directional short
in YES one fill at a time. Pre-match tennis takers buy YES; whoever quotes
the ask absorbs it. (The in-play sample was 78/22; cleaning it made the
imbalance worse, not better.)

## The typical fill loses, and so does the average

| horizon | n | mean | median | net median | positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60s | 27,754 | **−0.00104** | +0.0000 | −0.0025 | 29.1% |
| 300s | 31,480 | **−0.00256** | +0.0000 | −0.0025 | 32.4% |
| 1800s | 33,607 | **−0.00645** | +0.0000 | −0.0025 | 34.4% |

Median mark-out is zero at every horizon, so net median is exactly minus
the maker fee. The mean is **negative** at every horizon and gets worse
with time held: the maker is picked off, and the longer the inventory
sits, the more it costs. Fewer than a third of fills ever see the price
move the maker's way.

## Against the theoretical capture

Effective spread, measured as the median price change when the taker side
flips: **0.0100** (n=3,316 pre-match). So a passive leg could earn at most
**0.00500/contract** with zero adverse selection.

    theoretical half-spread   +0.00500
    realised mean (60s)       -0.00104
    realised median            0.00000
    maker fee                 -0.00250

Adverse selection takes more than the whole spread. The fee is half the
theoretical capture on its own.

## Caveats

- The maker fee rate (0.0025, ATP multiplier 1.0) is **not verified** —
  kalshi.com 429s on every fee-schedule URL. The rate moves the net rows;
  it does not move the 78/22 imbalance or the zero median.
- Marking to the next trade price understates capture when consecutive
  trades print on the same side. This is deliberate and conservative, and
  the fill imbalance stands regardless.
- Tennis pre-match only. In-play, and other series, are unmeasured.
- The 10¢-jump rule for the pre-match cut is a heuristic. It errs toward
  excluding late pre-match prints (a real 10¢ pre-match move is rare but
  possible), which biases *against* finding adverse selection — the
  result survives it anyway.

## The timestamp bug this uncovered

`sportsbot/exchanges/kalshi.py` set `MarketInfo.start_time` from
`occurrence_datetime`, and `bot/risk.py` uses `start_time` for the "no
entries within 10 minutes of start" guard. On tennis that guard was
pointed at the **end** of the match: the bot could enter mid-match with
the clock saying play had not begun. The same guard fell back to
`close_time`, which on Kalshi is a +2-week legal bound, so an unknown
start passed too.

Fixed fail-closed, per the repo's risk rule:

- Tennis markets carry no start (`start_time = None`) because Kalshi
  gives none — tennis tickers have no time of day.
- MLB's start is parsed from the ticker: `KXMLBGAME-26SEP222210…` is Sep
  22 22:10 **US Eastern**. Verified on 250/250 settled markets:
  `occurrence_datetime` is this time + 3h exactly, and the tape's first
  >10¢ move lands a median 15 minutes after it. (An earlier check read
  the ticker as Pacific and happened to coincide with `occurrence` — a
  timezone error; with `occurrence` as the start, MLB's guard was aimed
  at the final out.)
- An unknown start is **refused**, never proxied by `close_time`.
- A price-based in-play detector backstops the clock on every venue:
  the first mid the bot sees for a market is persisted, and an entry is
  refused once the mid has moved more than `risk.max_pre_match_move`
  (default 0.08) from it. A pre-match line rarely moves 8¢ (MLB mean
  move 24h→3h was 2.2¢); a live one does in minutes.

The practical effect is that the bot will not enter tennis pre-match on
Kalshi at all, which is what the numbers above say it should do.

## MLB: the same result at half the fee

Run on 250 most recent settled KXMLBGAME events, full trade tapes, maker
fee at the series multiplier (0.5 → 0.00125/contract). Three windows,
because the timestamp question matters here too:

| window | markets | fills | buy share | mean @60s | mean @30m | median | net median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all trades before `occurrence` | 244 | 1,695,145 | 23.2% | +0.0026 | +0.0033 | 0.0000 | −0.00125 |
| ≥3h before `occurrence` | 199 | 190,518 | **5.3%** | +0.0016 | +0.0042 | 0.0000 | −0.00125 |
| tape-defined (before first >10¢ move) | 245 | 276,303 | **7.9%** | +0.0003 | **−0.0024** | 0.0000 | −0.00125 |

Effective spread 1.0¢ in every window; theoretical half-spread capture
0.5¢; realised median 0.0¢. Verdict on all three: **NO EDGE**.

The flow is more one-sided than tennis, not less. In the genuinely
pre-game windows only **5–8% of maker fills are buys** — pre-game MLB
takers almost exclusively buy YES. Whoever quotes the ask absorbs the
whole flow; the bid is never hit. On the tape-defined window the mean
turns negative with holding time (+0.0003 → −0.0024), the same
picked-off-harder-the-longer-you-hold shape as tennis.

The half fee does not help: it moves net median from −0.0025 to −0.00125.
The problem is a zero median, and halving the fee on zero is still a loss.

That is nine hypotheses measured, nine negative.

Nine hypotheses, nine negative, all on real venue data. The repo's
models lose to the line and its passive quotes lose to the flow.

`sportsbot/backtest/markout.py` makes this repeatable: any future quoting
idea gets marked against a real tape, with the imbalance and the median
reported, before anyone funds it.
