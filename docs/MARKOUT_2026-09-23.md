# Is passive quoting profitable? No.

_2026-09-23_

Spread capture was the last hypothesis standing: it is structural rather
than predictive, so it does not require out-forecasting the line that
every model in this repo loses to. It does not pay either.

## Sample

500,170 pre-match trades across 207 settled Kalshi tennis markets
(KXATPMATCH + KXWTAMATCH). Only trades strictly before
`occurrence_datetime` — in-play is a different regime and this repo has
already been bitten by mixing them (see `VS_MARKET_2026-09-23.md`).

Kalshi's tape says which resting order was filled: `taker_book_side ==
"ask"` means the taker sold YES into a resting bid, so the maker bought.
Fills are marked to a later **trade** price rather than a mid — the maker
is credited only at a price someone actually dealt at, not one it might
not be able to exit against.

## The flow is 78/22

| | fills | share |
|---|---|---|
| maker SOLD yes (taker bought) | 391,590 | **0.783** |
| maker BOUGHT yes (taker sold) | 108,580 | 0.217 |

This is the finding that matters, and it is robust to every marking
choice. A maker quoting both sides gets hit **3.6× more on one side**.
That is not spread capture with a flat book — it is accumulating a large
directional short in YES, one fill at a time. Pre-match tennis takers buy
YES; whoever quotes the ask absorbs it.

It also invalidates the pooled average. Drift cancels between buys and
sells only when the two are balanced. At 78/22 it does not, which is why
the naive pooled mean looks positive (+0.00153 at 60s) while buy and sell
mark-outs diverge in opposite directions as the horizon grows (at 1800s:
buys −0.00938, sells +0.01099). That spread is price drift, not earnings.

## The typical fill captures nothing

| horizon | n | mean | median | net mean | net median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60s | 485,504 | +0.00153 | **+0.00000** | −0.00097 | **−0.00250** |
| 300s | 493,747 | +0.00270 | **+0.00000** | +0.00020 | **−0.00250** |
| 900s | 497,056 | +0.00393 | **+0.00000** | +0.00143 | **−0.00250** |

Median mark-out is exactly zero at every horizon, so net median is exactly
minus the maker fee. The positive mean is a fat tail: at 60s the split is
42.2% positive, 18.6% exactly zero, 39.2% negative, with p25 −0.02 against
p75 +0.02 — near-symmetric. Many fills capturing nothing and a few large
winners is the opposite of a market maker's profile.

## Against the theoretical capture

Effective spread, measured as the median price change when the taker side
flips: **0.0100** (n=72,626). So a passive leg could earn at most
**0.00500/contract** with zero adverse selection.

    theoretical half-spread   +0.00500
    realised median            0.00000
    maker fee                 -0.00250

Adverse selection takes the whole spread at the median. The fee is half
the theoretical capture on its own.

## Caveats

- The maker fee rate (0.0025, ATP multiplier 1.0) is **not verified** —
  kalshi.com 429s on every fee-schedule URL. The rate moves the net rows;
  it does not move the 78/22 imbalance or the zero median.
- Marking to the next trade price understates capture when consecutive
  trades print on the same side. This is deliberate and conservative, and
  the fill imbalance stands regardless.
- Tennis pre-match only. In-play, and other series, are unmeasured.

Eight hypotheses, eight negative, all on real venue data. The repo's
models lose to the line and its passive quotes lose to the flow.

`sportsbot/backtest/markout.py` makes this repeatable: any future quoting
idea gets marked against a real tape, with the imbalance and the median
reported, before anyone funds it.
