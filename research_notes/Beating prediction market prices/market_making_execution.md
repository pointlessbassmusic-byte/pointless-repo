# Execution-side edges in prediction markets: passive market making, rebates, adverse selection, queue, latency (through October 2026)

Scope note: this covers Polymarket (global CLOB and Polymarket US/QCX), Kalshi, and the academic/practitioner evidence on whether a $100–$5,000 account can earn through execution rather than prediction. Every figure is dated where the source dates it. Claims from vendor/SEO blogs and anonymous practitioners are explicitly flagged as unverified. Research performed 2026-10-07.

## Key Question 1: Polymarket maker rebates and liquidity rewards — exact 2026 terms, how computed, realistic $/day for small size, public ledgers

### Takeaway
Polymarket now runs two distinct maker programs: (a) **Maker Rebates**, a pro-rata share (15–25% by category; sports = 15%, crypto = 20%) of taker fees collected in each market, paid daily; and (b) **Liquidity Rewards**, a per-market daily pool allocated by a quadratic distance-from-midpoint score sampled once a minute, with single-sided quoting penalized 3x and disallowed outside the 0.10–0.90 midpoint band. Both pay only after $1 accrues. No official per-wallet ledger or Dune dashboard of reward payouts was found; the only available small-account estimates are vendor blogs and are unverified.

### Cited Findings

**Maker Rebates program (official)**
- Current official table (fetched 2026-10-07): maker fee 0 in every category; maker rebate share of taker fees = Crypto 20%, **Sports 15%**, Finance/Politics/Mentions/Tech 25%, Economics/Culture/Weather/Other 25%; Geopolitics fee-free (no rebate). Taker fee rates: Crypto 0.07, Sports 0.05, Finance/Politics/Mentions/Tech 0.04, Economics/Culture/Weather/Other 0.05. — [Polymarket docs: Maker Rebates Program](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/maker-rebates-program); same table on [Polymarket docs: Fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/fees)
- Fee/rebate formula: fee-equivalent = C × feeRate × p × (1 − p), C = shares, p = price; symmetric around 0.50; rounded to 5 decimals, min 0.00001 pUSD. Daily rebate = (your fee-equivalent / total fee-equivalent in that market) × rebate pool; computed per market, so makers compete only with other makers in the same market. Paid daily in pUSD; $1 minimum accrual before payout. Eligibility requires that your resting order be *filled* by a taker (resting alone earns nothing under this program). "The rebate percentage is at the sole discretion of Polymarket and may change over time." — [Polymarket docs: Maker Rebates Program](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/maker-rebates-program)
- Rollout history: taker fees and maker rebates began 2026-02-18 00:00 UTC for new NCAAB and Serie A markets with first payout 2026-02-19; the docs table at that time listed sports rebate at 25%. — [Polymarket docs (Feb 2026 version, via search snippet)](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/maker-rebates-program)
- Sacra: "Fee Structure V2" effective 2026-03-30 with sports taker 0.03; taker fees began Jan 2026 for high-frequency crypto, expanded to select sports Feb 2026, broader schedule Mar 2026. — [Sacra: Polymarket](https://www.sacra.com/c/polymarket/)
- Third-party (affiliate site, unverified against an official changelog): "As of July 2026, the sports fee rate rose from 0.03 to 0.05 ... The sports maker rebate share also fell from 25% to 15%." The current official page is consistent with the post-July figures (0.05 / 15%). — [startpolymarket.com fees](https://startpolymarket.com/learn/polymarket-fees/); also [laikalabs](https://laikalabs.ai/prediction-markets/market-making-on-polymarket)
- Worked arithmetic (mine, from the official formula): at p = 0.50 on a sports market, taker fee = 0.05 × 0.25 = 1.25¢ per share; the maker-side rebate pool is 15% of that = **0.1875¢ per share filled**, i.e. ~0.19% of notional at mid-price, falling to 0.09¢ at p = 0.20/0.80. — derived from [Polymarket docs: Fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/fees)

**Liquidity Rewards program (official methodology)**
- Order book sampled once per minute at a random offset; an epoch is one UTC day (up to 1,440 samples). Parameters: v = max qualifying spread from midpoint (cents), s = order's spread from the size-cutoff-adjusted midpoint, c = scaling factor currently 3.0 on all markets, b = in-game multiplier (values not published). Order score S(v,s) = ((v − s)/v)² · b. Q_one and Q_two aggregate bid-side and ask-side scores (including the complementary token). For midpoint in [0.10, 0.90]: Q_min = max(min(Q_one, Q_two), max(Q_one/c, Q_two/c)) (single-sided liquidity counts at 1/3). For midpoint outside that band: Q_min = min(Q_one, Q_two) (must be two-sided). Q_normal = Q_min / Σ Q_min across makers in the sample; Q_epoch = Σ Q_normal over samples; Q_final = Q_epoch / Σ Q_epoch across makers × market reward pool. Paid daily at midnight UTC; minimum payout $1. Each market sets its own min qualifying size, max spread and reward allocation (numbers live in per-market settings, not on the methodology page). — [Polymarket docs: Liquidity Rewards (market-makers)](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)
- Worked example in the docs (midpoint 0.50, v = 3¢): bids of 100 @ 1¢ and 200 @ 2¢ plus 100 complementary asks @ 1¢ give Q_one ≈ 111.1; the other side ≈ 175.0; Q_min = 111.1. Note the quadratic: an order 1¢ off a 3¢ max spread scores (2/3)² = 0.44 per share, an order 2¢ off scores (1/3)² = 0.11, i.e. being 2x tighter earns 4x. — [Polymarket docs: Liquidity Rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)
- Program budgets that were official: a Crypto TWAP program with a $1M August allocation (5-min $550k, 15-min $350k, 4-hour $100k) is marked "This program has ended." — [Polymarket docs: Liquidity Rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)
- Sports-specific programs (official docs via search snippets): "Polymarket is distributing over $5M in liquidity incentives for April 2026 across sports and esports markets. The reward pool is split into Pre (pre-game) and Live (in-play) periods per game." and a World Cup 2026 program 2026-06-11 to 2026-07-19 with per-game Pre/Live caps; amounts "are reward caps, and actual payouts depend on eligible quoting." The current methodology page (fetched 2026-10-07) lists **no** active sports/esports program, only the ended crypto TWAP program. — [Polymarket docs: Liquidity Rewards (programs)](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/liquidity-rewards)
- Perps liquidity rewards (separate product): $75,000/day pool split evenly across active Perps markets, divided among makers by score; requires ≥1% of trailing 7-day maker volume to qualify; 12:00–12:00 UTC periods, paid in pUSD. The 1% threshold effectively excludes small accounts. — [Polymarket docs: Perps Liquidity Rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/perps/liquidity-rewards)

### Inferences
- The Maker Rebate at 15% of a 0.05 × p(1−p) taker fee is worth at most ~0.19% of notional per filled share on sports. For a $1,000 account turning over its capital once a day as a maker (i.e. $1,000 filled/day), that is **~$1.90/day gross** before any adverse-selection loss — essentially the $1 daily minimum payout threshold. The rebate cannot by itself carry a small account; it only slightly offsets adverse selection.
- Liquidity Rewards are a share-of-pool game: payout scales with your share of Σ Q_min across *all* makers in that market every minute. Because the score is linear in size and quadratic in tightness, a $500 quoter against makers quoting thousands of shares at the inside receives a proportionally tiny slice; and 2x tighter quoting (4x score) is exactly what maximizes adverse-selection exposure.
- The evidence that Polymarket's sports pools were time-boxed campaigns (April 2026, World Cup June 11–July 19) and that the methodology page currently lists no active sports program means any 2026 $/day estimate built on sports pools is stale by October 2026.

- I could not confirm from an official source the exact date the sports rebate dropped from 25% to 15% (only affiliate sites say July 2026).

## Key Question 2: Kalshi — maker fees on game series, rebate/liquidity programs, API rate limits, documented market-maker behavior

### Takeaway
Kalshi charges a taker fee of 0.07 × C × P × (1−P) and, on designated series (including major sports game series), a maker fee of 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P) rounded up to the cent — a maker *pays* up to 0.44¢/contract rather than receiving a rebate. Its open Liquidity Incentive Program pays $1–$1,000 per market per day from one-second random book snapshots with a discount-factor-by-tick score and a 100–20,000 contract target size that must rest on *both* sides; the designated Market Maker Program (Susquehanna first) is gated. Base API tier is 10 writes/second; higher tiers require 0.075%+ of 30-day exchange volume.

### Cited Findings

**Fees**
- Kalshi's published schedule (reported last updated 2026-02-05): general taker fee = round-up(0.07 × C × P × (1−P)); maker fee = 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P), rounded up to the next cent, "charged for orders placed that are not immediately matched and are instead left as resting orders," charged only on execution, no cancel fee; "Maker fees are always 25% of the taker fee." Which markets carry maker fees is listed at kalshi.com/fee-schedule. — [sailgp.com Kalshi fees (secondary, quoting the PDF)](https://sailgp.com/prediction-markets/kalshi/fees); [pm.wiki Kalshi fees explained](https://pm.wiki/uk/learn/kalshi-fees-explained). Direct fetch of the official PDF/fee page returned a Vercel security checkpoint / HTTP 429 on 2026-10-07, so these coefficients are from secondary sources quoting it.
- Pre-July-1 (2025) maker fee was a flat 0.25¢ per $1 contract; replaced by the percentage formula that peaks at 50¢. A comparison chart covers NHL, NBA and NFL markets under both formulas. — [ingame.com Kalshi fee change for sports](https://www.ingame.com/kalshis-change-may-increase-fee-sports-traders/); [Allium: why cost peaks near 50 cents](https://allium.so/blog/kalshi-fees-why-the-cost-peaks-near-50-cents/)
- Kalshi help: "in some cases, markets will have maker fees associated with them"; special fees for elections, awards, major sports championships (page dated 2026-04-19). — [Kalshi Help: Fees](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823805-fees)
- The series-level API field `fee_type` takes values `quadratic`, `quadratic_with_maker_fees`, `quadratic_with_combo_maker_fees`, `margin_market_maker_program_fees`; per-trade maker fee = M_maker × 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P) with the maker multiplier default 0 (i.e., `quadratic` series charge no maker fee). This is documented by data vendor Allium, which ingests the Kalshi series API. — [Allium Kalshi series docs](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series); [Allium series fee changes](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series-fee-changes)
- Third-party guide: maker fees of 25% of taker apply on "about a hundred named exception series, such as certain economics, sports, and awards markets"; most other markets have no maker fee. — [botforkalshi fees explained](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained) (unverified count)
- Bartlett & O'Hara (academic): "Kalshi charges taker fees on all markets, ranging from 0.07¢ to 1.75¢ per contract depending on the price level and market type. Starting in April 2025, makers on some market types also face fees ranging from 0.02¢ to 0.44¢ per contract." — [Bartlett & O'Hara, NBER conf. paper, July 2, 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Perpetual futures (CFTC filing June 2026): volume-tiered maker fees 5.0 bps base down to 0.6 bps at the top tier; tiers by 30-day trailing volume across perps and prediction contracts; effective no earlier than 2026-06-22. — [CFTC filing rules0608265590](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules0608265590.pdf)

**Liquidity / rebate programs**
- Liquidity Incentive Program (CFTC filing, July 15, 2026 update, effective July 30, 2026): applies to all Kalshi markets; runs "until the earlier of January 1, 2027, or the date that Kalshi amends or terminates"; eligible = all members except Kalshi affiliates, **members who have executed a Market Maker Agreement**, and IBs/FCMs and their customers. Per market: a schedule of Time Periods (≤31 days) each with Target Size (>100 and <20,000 contracts), Discount Factor (≤1.00), and Time Period Reward (≥$1, ≤$1,000 per calendar day). Score per qualifying bid = DiscountFactor^N × size, N = ticks below the Reference Price (N = 0 if at/above reference); normalized across participants per snapshot; averaged over the period; payout scaled by non-excluded/total snapshots. — [CFTC filing rules07152610358 (Kalshi LIP July 2026 update)](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf)
- Help-center details: book sampled once per second at a random moment; Reference Price = first level from the best bid where cumulative resting size reaches one-fifth of Target Size; a snapshot is excluded if resting orders don't meet the Target Size on **both** yes and no sides; a snapshot is worth at most 2.0 across all participants; example: $100 period, 10,000 snapshots, 8,000 qualifying, 20% share → $16.00. Rewards "$1 to $1,000 per market per day", $1.00 minimum payout; end date January 1, 2027; international non-U.S. users cannot earn rewards; verified SSN required above IRS thresholds. — [Kalshi Help: Liquidity Incentive Program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)
- Feb 11, 2026 amendment excluded snapshots lacking qualifying Yes or No bids. — [CFTC filing rules02112639183](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/filings/orgrules/26/02/rules02112639183.pdf)
- Designated Liquidity Provider program: members with a Market Maker Agreement can bid in periodic "auctions" by stating the minimum reward they would accept to meet quoting requirements for an Incentivized Series; all active programs list termination 12/31/26. Provider counts per series are mostly 1 (e.g., Baseball 1, Football 1, each Tennis/Golf group 1), Crypto 15-min 2, perps 3–6 per coin. Quoting obligations and reward amounts are not published. — [Kalshi Help: Liquidity Provider Program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program)
- Market Maker Program: "highly selective," reviewed on financial resources, trading experience, reputation; benefits "reduced fees, differing position limits, and enhanced access"; obligations to quote continuous two-sided markets within spread/size limits. 2024 CFTC filing said the program ran until March 11, 2026 unless extended. — [Kalshi Help: How to become a market maker](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823819-how-to-become-a-market-maker-on-kalshi); [CFTC filing rules02262412176](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules02262412176.pdf)
- Volume Incentive Program (Aug 2025 filing): weekly tiered rebates "like tax brackets" from 0.500% at $12,500 weekly volume to 1.000% at $312,500; only CLOB trades priced $0.03–$0.97 qualify; Market Maker Agreement members and affiliates excluded; effective on/after 2025-08-22 until 2026-08-01 or amendment. — [Kalshi Volume and Liquidity Incentive Program Aug 2025 (PDF)](https://www.ingame.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Volume-and-Liquidity-Incentive-Program-August-2025.pdf); [Kalshi FAQ Volume Incentive Program](https://help.kalshi.com/faq/what-is-the-volume-incentive-program)

- CFTC concern: an advisory said market-maker arrangements that guarantee net profits or cover losses via rebates "may encourage artificial strategies"; Kalshi excludes fees from self-matching/wash/pre-arranged trading from rebate eligibility. Separately, Kalshi faced wash-trading allegations over crypto volume (disputed). — [CFTC filing rules0625267305](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules0625267305.pdf); [crypto.news on wash-trading claims](https://crypto.news/kalshi-faces-wash-trading-claims-over-crypto-volume/)

Also: "Brief quotes of about 0.1 seconds catch only about 10% of snapshots." — [UFO Holdings Substack](https://ufoholdings.substack.com/p/kalshis-new-liquidity-incentives)

**Documented market-maker behavior on Kalshi**
- Kalshi "runs two tiers of liquidity provision: a gated Market Maker Program ... (the Wall Street Journal reports Susquehanna International Group as the first such firm), and an open Liquidity Incentive Program that rewards ordinary" members; the LIP "excludes designated market makers." — [Bartlett & O'Hara 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- "The institutional makers that did enter, Susquehanna foremost, came from sports-betting" backgrounds. — [Bartlett & O'Hara 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Makers overwhelmingly hold to settlement: on Polymarket's on-chain record, "the median maker position is carried to settlement in full, and 92% of positions in markets resolving on a single event ... are held to resolution"; across 97,766 Polymarket crypto price markets (Jan–Mar 2026), "makers carried 95% of gross volume to settlement ... and 82% even in the most balanced markets." Kalshi's netting rule "on a one-cent spread recovers almost nothing." — [Bartlett & O'Hara 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)

### Inferences
- On a Kalshi game series with maker fees, a resting order at 50¢ pays 0.0175 × 0.25 = 0.4375¢ → rounded **up to 1¢** per contract for small clips (cent rounding), i.e. a 1-cent spread captured at 50¢ is fully consumed by the maker fee on a 1-contract fill; the rounding penalty shrinks only with larger clips (100 contracts → 44¢ ≈ 0.44¢/contract). This is a direct structural disadvantage to small-size quoting on those series.
- The LIP's "both sides must meet Target Size (>100 contracts)" exclusion means that in a thin market a solo small quoter can't even create qualifying snapshots alone; and because score is normalized per snapshot, a $1,000 account quoting 20 contracts against a 1,000-contract target earns at most ~2% of the per-market reward (≤$20/day at the $1,000 cap, typically far less given the $1–$1,000 range).
- Basic-tier 10 writes/sec is adequate for quoting a handful of markets but not for sub-second cancel/replace across dozens of live games; Expert tier needs ~0.075% of exchange volume (hundreds of thousands of dollars/month at 2026 volumes), out of reach for a small account.

## Key Question 3: Adverse selection on binary contracts — measured post-fill mark-outs and variation with time-to-event / news

### Takeaway
The best measurement is Bartlett & O'Hara (41.6M Kalshi trades, July 2026 draft): makers earn a volume-weighted **+1.91¢/contract on single-name markets and +0.82¢ on broad-based** despite significantly higher informed price impact, because a YES-overbetting "behavioral surplus" (maker win rate 63.4% vs 53.8% predicted) cross-subsidizes adverse selection; losses are concentrated in the top trailing-VPIN tail (VPIN > 0.94: −$68/bucket, 59.4% loss rate). On Polymarket, Dubach (600 markets, 6.4M trades, Feb–Apr 2026) finds the top-100 markets' Glosten-Harris adverse-selection component is ~0.0 in probability points, and a paper-trading fill-model author measured +1.13¢ 1-minute mark-out (all spread capture, drift ≈ +0.13¢) on sports O/U quotes. Nobody has published mark-outs by time-to-event; the live-sports news-shock exposure (15–30¢ jumps on goals) is only described, not measured.

### Cited Findings

**Bartlett & O'Hara, "Adverse Selection in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Kalshi" (working paper, v. July 2, 2026; first version April 16, 2026)**
- Data: 41.6M trades across 478,167 markets; maker P&L computed exactly from taker-side flag and $0/$1 settlement, assuming hold-to-settlement; fees ignored (so maker profits are overstated). — [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Single-name markets show higher informed price impact (Kyle's λ in log-odds, t = 10.88, p < 0.001); Glosten-Harris shows the impact is "almost entirely permanent (information, not inventory), with the transitory component economically negligible." — [same](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Table 5 (settled markets with ≥1 trade; 14,988 single-name / 460,658 broad-based; 239.8M / 3.94B contracts): mean maker P&L **1.91¢ vs 0.82¢ per contract**; maker win rate 63.4% vs 53.9%; average win 16.34¢ vs 24.21¢; average loss −23.13¢ vs −26.59¢; % markets maker-profitable 66.5% vs 49.4%; mean maker P&L/market $305.89 vs $69.89; median $4.48 vs −$0.01; total maker P&L $4.58M vs $32.2M. — [same, Table 5](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Decomposition: single-name frequency edge +5.31¢ minus magnitude deficit −3.40¢; broad-based +2.01¢ − 1.19¢. Predicted single-name win rate under independence 53.8% vs actual 63.4% (9.6pp behavioral gap). — [same, Table 6](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Time series: frequency edge rose from ~0 in early 2024 to ~+8¢/contract by early 2026 while the magnitude deficit widened to ~−6¢; net profit stayed in a 1–3¢ band. — [same](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Case studies: Bezos Super Bowl market (888,006 contracts, YES fell 70¢→30¢ on fraternity-network informed NO buying) — makers still netted $29,274 (3.3¢/contract) because 518,000 uninformed YES contracts at avg 19¢ outweighed 370,000 informed NO contracts. Wahlberg market: takers 58.9% YES-biased at avg 29¢; makers earned $1.18M. — [same](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Real-time toxicity: trailing VPIN (20-bucket) at interior prices (30–70¢): single-name maker P&L/bucket +$41 (Q1, VPIN 0.54), +$46 (Q4, 0.80), **−$12 (Q5, 0.90)** with loss rate 43.1% → 51.7%; finer bins: positive below VPIN ≈ 0.86, **−$68/bucket and 59.4% loss rate above 0.94**. Regression β = −139 (t = −2.42). Broad-based: no predictive power (P&L $4–$11 flat). Mechanism: one-sided YES flow in high-VPIN single-name buckets predicts YES settlement. — [same, Tables 9–10](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Category ranking (Internet Appendix B): single-name highest informed price impact, broad-based lowest, "politics, sports, entertainment, weather, and other markets falling in between." Cites concurrent work (Palumbo 2026) that Kalshi NFL market making "resembles underwriting more than intermediation." — [same](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)

### Inferences
- Kalshi maker economics are net positive in aggregate (0.8–1.9¢/contract gross of fees) but *thin*: Kalshi's maker fee on fee-bearing series (0.02–0.44¢) and the cent rounding can consume most of the broad-based 0.82¢ edge for small clips. The edge is not spread capture but underwriting the YES-bias of retail takers, which requires (a) holding to settlement, hence capital locked and full-loss tail exposure, and (b) being on the right side of flow composition, which a small account cannot diversify across thousands of markets.
- The Bartlett–O'Hara VPIN result is the only validated real-time toxicity signal: makers should pull quotes when trailing one-sided flow exceeds ~0.86–0.94 on single-name/event markets; on crypto/index markets the signal is useless.
- Dubach's "no detectable adverse-selection component" in Polymarket's top-100 markets plus the fill-model's zero post-fill drift suggest that at the 1-minute horizon, in liquid non-event windows, the adverse-selection cost of resting on Polymarket is small; the cost is concentrated in discrete news events (goals, resolutions), which the hold-to-settlement data captures as the "magnitude deficit" (losses ~40% larger than wins per contract).

### Gaps
- No study reports mark-outs by time-to-event or by news-flow intensity for sports contracts; the in-play loss-per-goal figures are vendor guesses.
- No published fill rates (% of resting orders filled) for Polymarket or Kalshi; the only data point is the fill-model's 0.105 trades/market-hour under conservative queue assumptions.
- Palumbo (2026) on Kalshi NFL market making and Burgi/Deng/Whelan (2026) are cited by Bartlett & O'Hara but I could not retrieve them.

## Key Question 4: Latency / lead-lag execution between venues and whether resting-order strategies captured it

### Takeaway
Measured lead-lag work exists for Polymarket vs Binance (median ~16 ms cross-venue lag with ±99 ms clock uncertainty; Polymarket quotes react to large Binance moves after a median ~347 ms; ML trading model null) and for political venues (Polymarket leads Kalshi; Polymarket + Betfair ≈ 85% of Hasbrouck information share; sportsbooks excluded from the model confidence set). No study measures Kalshi↔Polymarket or sportsbook→exchange lead-lag in *sports* or documents a resting-order strategy capturing it. The one documented latency edge — Polymarket's ~500 ms taker delay that let makers re-quote — was removed in February 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Polymarket vs Binance (single-author preprint, 28 Jul 2026): median cross-venue lag ≈ 16 ms on venue clocks, clock-offset uncertainty ≈ ±99 ms unresolved; event study on collector clock: Polymarket quotes react to large Binance moves after median ≈ 347 ms; lead-lag constant across disagreement levels; ML trading model built on the data produced a null result; 727M deduplicated rows, 54 Polymarket days between 2026-02-12 and 2026-05-15. — [alphaxiv 2607.26245](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)
- Ng, Peng, Tao & Zhou, "Price Discovery and Trading in Modern Prediction Markets" (SSRN, rev. 28 Apr 2026): 2024 election contracts on Polymarket, Kalshi, PredictIt, Robinhood; "Polymarket leads Kalshi in price discovery, particularly when liquidity and trading activity are high, implying economically meaningful arbitrage opportunities"; net order imbalance from large trades strongly predicts subsequent returns. No lag length in abstract. — [SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5331995)
- Aktuğ & Torul (April 2026 draft): nine political platforms, 2024 election; "PolyMarket and Betfair jointly account for nearly 85% of Hasbrouck information shares, and all sportsbooks are excluded from the Model Confidence Set"; leadership horizon-dependent (Polymarket at 5-min, Betfair at 1-hour). — [Boğaziçi pridis.pdf](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)
- Blockworks on Kalshi sports: prices track sportsbooks with correlations up to 0.996, but a 2.75% cost threshold and thin in-game liquidity leave few arbitrageable intervals, which are short-lived. (Research note, not peer-reviewed; does not establish lead direction.) — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Polymarket 500 ms taker delay: Polymarket imposed ~0.5 s delay on liquidity-removing orders, giving makers a window to adjust quotes on 5-minute BTC markets; one trader called it "basically free money"; observers noticed removal in February 2026 with no announcement; Polymarket "reportedly still gives maker fee rebates." — [Protos, Feb 20, 2026](https://protos.com/polymarket-ends-trading-loophole-for-bitcoin-quants/)
- Self-reported bot operator (dev.to, unverified): after the Feb 2026 delay removal and dynamic taker fees (up to ~1.56% on 5/15-min crypto), rolling 7-day EV per trade stayed ~$0.028–0.031 at 78 trades/day using maker-side limit orders; entry threshold raised from 70¢ to 72¢; no account size or dollar PnL disclosed. — [dev.to 1kto1m](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)
- Infrastructure: Glassnode runs a public latency probe to Polymarket and Kalshi infrastructure from multiple locations (monitoring tool, not a study). — [Glassnode hyperlatency probe](https://hyperlatency.glassnode.com/prediction-markets/status)
- Bot-builder guidance (not a requirement): sub-100–200 ms end-to-end cancel/replace loop, WebSocket rather than REST; poly-maker tracks per-fill mark-out EWMAs, has an EVENT regime that pulls quotes on sweeps/jumps and HALTED on stale data. — [warproxxx/poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)
- Within-Polymarket arbitrage (not cross-venue): Saguillo et al. (IMDEA, arXiv 5 Aug 2025) estimate ~$39.6M realized profit from market-rebalancing and combinatorial (NegRisk) arbitrage April 2024–April 2025; single-condition "buy below $1" ≈ $5.90M and "sell above $1" ≈ $4.68M; NegRisk buy-NO ≈ $17.31M; sports single markets dominate exploited single-condition opportunities while politics dominates NegRisk profit; multi-leg arbitrage is non-atomic (one leg can fill while another fails); some large players show "bot-like behaviour." — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1)
- A Substack primer cites a "Clinton and Huang (2025)" cross-platform divergence study that I could not verify. — [decryptedbydev Substack](https://decryptedbydev.substack.com/p/prediction-market-arbitrage-why-information) (unverified)

### Inferences
- The only latency edge documented as "free money" for makers (the 500 ms taker delay) was a venue design feature, since removed; after removal, makers on 5-min crypto face takers with sub-second Binance-informed signals (~16–350 ms lag), so resting quotes are structurally stale unless the maker also consumes the Binance feed.
- Cross-venue sports lead-lag (sportsbook → Kalshi/Polymarket) is plausible from the 0.996 correlation but unmeasured; Blockworks' 2.75% cost threshold implies that for a taker it is rarely profitable, and for a resting-order maker the "capture" would be the mirror image: being hit by takers who saw the sportsbook move first.
- Co-location and websockets are table stakes for in-play quoting but no source documents a small account winning on latency; winners in the IMDEA data were bot operators doing non-atomic multi-leg arbitrage at scale.

### Gaps
- No Kalshi↔Polymarket sports lead-lag measurement found; no Hasbrouck/information-share estimates for sports contracts.
- No documented infrastructure (co-location, feed handlers) used by profitable prediction-market makers beyond vendor recommendations.

## Key Question 6: Models — Avellaneda-Stoikov and Glosten-Milgrom applied to binary contracts; open-source bots with logs

### Takeaway
Only one quantitative Avellaneda-Stoikov adaptation to Polymarket was found (a 2026 Comillas master's thesis: Bernoulli variance replaces volatility, quotes bounded in [0,1]; backtest ≈ 155 USDC per market, profit mainly from spread, tail-event sensitive). Bartlett & O'Hara formalize the Glosten-Milgrom break-even for a hold-to-settlement maker: s + (1−α)σ ≥ α·ℓ, where the informed loss ℓ is the full distance to $0/$1. No open-source bot publishes audited live PnL; poly-maker (1.5k stars) explicitly has no backtester and warns it can lose money.

### Cited Findings
- Comillas thesis (2026): adapts A-S to Polymarket binaries; "quotes bid and ask prices within the bounded interval [0,1], replaces historical volatility with Bernoulli variance and validates the strategy through backtesting on real markets"; ≈155 USDC per market in the test set; most profit from spread capture; bid/ask activity roughly balanced; main weakness tail risk; author calls it "an open proposal with favorable historical validation, not ... a closed demonstration of real operation." Repository page returned 403/410 on direct fetch; details from search snippet. — [Comillas repository](https://repositorio.comillas.edu/jspui/handle/11531/109132?mode=full)
- Glosten-Milgrom break-even for prediction-market makers: with α = informed share of flow, ℓ = loss on an informed contract ("the full distance from the price to settlement"), σ = surplus on a quasi-informed contract, s = spread income per contract, the maker provides liquidity only if s + (1−α)σ ≥ α·ℓ; e.g. a maker selling YES at 57¢ to an informed buyer loses 43¢. The canonical round-trip is "available to a prediction-market maker too, but she typically does not complete it." — [Bartlett & O'Hara 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)
- Open-source bots: poly-maker (maker-only, post-only, political markets; depth-weighted microprice + signed-flow EWMA; inventory skew with soft cap; regimes QUIET/TRENDING/EVENT/REDUCE_ONLY/HALTED; per-market notional cap, total exposure cap, neg-risk worst-case cap, daily-loss kill switch, WS-staleness halt; 1.5k stars, 489 forks; "backtester not yet built"; no PnL reported). — [warproxxx/poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker). Polypigeon (A-S + inventory mgmt, slippage-modeled paper trading, no figures) — [polypigeon mirror](https://backiee.wasmer.app/https_github_com/CMLKevin/polypigeon); bs-p (A-S in logit space, ~100 stars, no performance claims) — [oosmetrics bs-p](https://oosmetrics.com/repo/lubluniky/bs-p).
- A "24/7 market making desk" guide and bot-seller pages claiming e.g. $171k/month offer no verified results. — [youmind guide](https://youmind.com/landing/x-viral-articles/build-ai-market-making-desk) (promotional, disregard)
- "Axiomatic Market Making" (arXiv 2606.09454) and "Market Informedness and Market-Maker Profitability" (arXiv 2606.05882) appeared in search as theory on the adverse-selection/price-discovery trade-off; not read in full. — [arXiv 2606.05882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05882)

### Inferences
- The binding adaptation of A-S to binaries is not the reservation-price formula but the terminal condition: σ² → p(1−p) collapses toward zero near 0/1 while the jump-to-settlement risk is maximal, so inventory penalties must scale with time-to-resolution and news hazard, not Bernoulli variance alone. Bartlett–O'Hara's hold-to-settlement evidence implies real makers are closer to a Glosten-Milgrom underwriter than an A-S inventory-neutral dealer.

### Gaps
- No live, audited PnL log from any open-source prediction-market maker bot was found.
- Thesis full text not retrieved; number of markets in its test set unknown.

### Inferences
- For a US-resident small account, the only compliant execution venues are Kalshi and Polymarket US; Polymarket US is the only one of the three with a true *per-fill maker rebate paid regardless of pool share* (−0.0125 × p(1−p) ≈ 0.31¢/contract at 50¢ per Sacra), making it the most favorable fee structure for small passive size — but it is iOS-app/KYC gated and its API availability for quoting is undocumented in sources found.
- Resolution/clarification risk is a maker-specific tail: a maker holding paired inventory is hedged, but a maker holding a one-sided residual into a disputed resolution faces a full-loss outcome with no recourse; this risk is absent on Kalshi (centralized settlement) but present on Polymarket global.

### Gaps
- Could not find Polymarket US API/CLOB documentation for programmatic quoting, nor the current maker-rebate coefficient in the July 2026 CFTC exhibit.
- No documented case of a market-maker wallet specifically harmed by a UMA dispute (the reported losers were directional traders).
- Current authoritative state-availability list for Polymarket US not found.

## Overall assessment: can a $100–$5,000 account earn consistently on execution alone?

### Takeaway
The aggregate evidence says passive liquidity provision on binary markets is positive-EV in aggregate but very thin (≈0.8–1.9¢/contract gross on Kalshi before fees; ~2% per pair for the dominant Polymarket sports maker), extremely concentrated (0.55% of profitable maker wallets take half the maker profit), queue-dominated (best-bid queues of 600–800 shares; a 5-share order sits ~120th), and tail-exposed (losses ~40% larger than wins per contract; single VPIN-tail buckets at −$68). Rebates at small size are near the $1/day payout floor. No independently verified case of a $100–$5,000 account earning consistently through execution was found; the one small paper-traded experiment could not distinguish its +4.5% from zero and estimated ~1,400 resolutions (~70 days at 20/day) just to tell a modest gain from a modest loss.

### Inferences
- Realistic gross expectation for a $1,000–$5,000 two-sided quoter in liquid sports/crypto markets, using the measured per-contract edges (~1–2¢/contract on fills, Kalshi; ~2% per completed pair, Polymarket) and plausible fill rates from the fill-model (order of 0.1 fills/market-hour at the back of the queue across a handful of markets), is low single-digit dollars per day before adverse-selection tail losses and fees, with a standard deviation per position of ~125% (fill-model estimate). This is consistent with "learning-sized," not income-sized, outcomes.
- The execution edges that demonstrably paid in 2025–2026 (500 ms taker delay on crypto, time-boxed sports reward pools, NegRisk/combinatorial arbitrage at scale, pair-minting with queue dominance) were either removed, campaign-limited, or require capital and queue position a small account cannot obtain.
- If a small account pursues execution edges anyway, the evidence favors: Polymarket US or Kalshi non-maker-fee series (avoid 0.0175 series at small clip sizes due to cent rounding); two-sided pair-minting rather than one-sided quoting; pulling quotes when trailing one-sided flow exceeds ~0.86 (Bartlett–O'Hara VPIN threshold) on event markets; and never carrying one-sided residuals into Polymarket global resolutions subject to UMA clarification risk.

### Gaps
- No verified small-account live PnL ledger (maker-only) exists in any source found; all small-account statements are vendor opinion or paper-trading.
- No published fill-rate distribution by queue position for either venue.
- No official statement from Polymarket on whether general (non-campaign) liquidity rewards remain funded for sports as of October 2026.
