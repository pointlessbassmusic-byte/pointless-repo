# Sharp Sportsbook Lines and External Data Feeds as "Fair Value" Against Polymarket / Kalshi Sports Prices

Research date: 2026-10-07. All prices/limits are as published on the date fetched (noted inline). "Vendor" = the provider's own marketing or a competitor's comparison page; "measured" = a dataset with reported sample size and realised results.

## Key Question 1: Is "Pinnacle (or Circa/Betfair) devigged line vs Polymarket/Kalshi price" a documented edge? What CLV/ROI, at what lead times?

### Takeaway
"Pinnacle closing line as fair value" is well documented as an edge *against other sportsbooks* (realised ROI +1.8% to +3.7% at 0-2% EV thresholds over 5k-31k bets in golf and European football), and that edge appears to have shrunk sharply since 2023/24. Against Polymarket/Kalshi specifically there is **no peer-reviewed or independently measured CLV/ROI study**; the evidence is one retired open-source bot (+$5k net over a few months in early 2026, with the directional residual losing money), vendor blogs asserting a 3-8% divergence, and industry analyses showing Kalshi prices track FanDuel at up to 0.996 correlation while being worse than FanDuel/DraftKings once fees are counted.

### Cited Findings

**Pinnacle-as-benchmark: measured evidence (vs other books, not vs exchanges)**
- Data Golf (Dec 18, 2020), 106,204 golf matchups/3-balls from 11 books since Jan 2019: blind (margin-free) returns ~0% at every book; betting other books using Pinnacle closing odds as fair value with 1-unit stakes gave realised ROI +1.84% at a 0% EV threshold (4,803 bets), +3.07% at 1% (3,040 bets), +3.75% at 2% (1,902 bets), falling to -0.59% at 5% (511 bets). Expected-vs-realised ROI was ~1:1 for Pinnacle but only weakly positive for DraftKings closing odds. Random betting at Pinnacle margin-free odds over 5,000 bets gives a 5th-95th percentile range of roughly -5.9% to -1.6%, so 2-3% deviations are possible even at 10,000 bets. — [Data Golf, "How sharp are bookmakers?"](https://datagolf.com/analyzing-betting-odds)
- Networked Substack analysis of Buchdahl's football-data.co.uk (22 European leagues, ~20 books, 193,803 matches, 1,970,653 sets of 1X2 odds, 2000/01-2025/26): betting best-available odds where devigged Pinnacle implies 2-10% EV yielded, on pre-closing odds 2012/13-2025/26, 31,247 bets, expected ROI 3.8% vs realised 3.6%; on closing odds 2019/20 onward, 23,418 bets, expected 4.3% vs realised 3.5%; on closing odds 2023/24 onward, 6,806 bets, expected 4.2% vs realised **1.9%**. Pre-closing odds were collected Friday afternoons (weekend games) / Tuesday afternoons (midweek), closing = last before kickoff; no finer lead-time analysis. — [A view from the Pinnacle](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)
- Data Golf credits the method (use one book's devigged odds to estimate EV against another's) to Joseph Buchdahl. — [Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/analyzing-betting-odds)
- Buchdahl maintains football-data.co.uk and tennis-data.co.uk (free historical pre-closing/closing odds commonly used by academic researchers); I could not fetch tennis-data.co.uk (HTTP 403) to confirm current bookmaker columns. — [Search summary citing Buchdahl bio](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)
- Academic odds-as-forecast literature: Štrumbelj & Robnik Šikonja, "Online bookmakers' odds as forecasts: the case of European soccer leagues", Int. J. Forecasting 26(3) 2010, pp. 482-488; Štrumbelj, "On determining probability forecasts from betting odds", Int. J. Forecasting 30(4) 2014, pp. 934-943 (basis of the Shin devig method used in the `shin` Python package). Search results gave bibliographic data only, not whether Pinnacle was the data source. — [Univ. of Ljubljana](https://www.fri.uni-lj.si/en/node/1957); [mberk/shin](https://www.github.com/mberk/shin)
- Jay Simon, "Inefficient Forecasts at the Sportsbook: An Analysis of Real-Time Betting Line Movement", Management Science 2025 (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.00456): open-to-close line movement at four sportsbooks across 3,681 MLB games; forecasts "mostly reliable" but simple strategies would have profited; forecasts do not improve monotonically toward first pitch (weekend day games were worse at start than 90 minutes earlier); lines overreact with significantly negatively autocorrelated changes. Weak-form efficiency rejected. Data files available via the DOI supplement. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.00456)
- Karl Whelan's critique: beating the closing line does not guarantee long-run profit; NBA moneylines are an efficient market with no favourite-longshot bias. — [The Truth about Closing Line Value](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)
- An NFL/NBA study found betting against teams that have clinched playoff position at the opening line is consistently profitable; closing lines move in the expected direction but only partially. — [Anchoring, affect, and efficiency (Financial Services Review)](https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/view/3242)

**Prediction-market pricing vs sportsbooks: industry/vendor evidence (no CLV study found)**
- Blockworks Research (Kunal Doshi), "From Betting to Trading: How Kalshi is Reshaping Sports Markets": 3.1K games / $8.5B notional; implied probabilities deviate from outcomes by 5.5 pp on average measured two days before the event; real-time Kalshi prices track FanDuel with correlations "as high as 0.996"; a 2.75% cost threshold plus thin in-game liquidity leave few arbitrageable intervals and gaps are short-lived; analysis focused on basketball and NFL (72% of Kalshi sports volume); Kalshi monthly notional grew from $180M (early 2025) to $14.4B (March 2026), sports = 68% of volume. The full page returned 404 on fetch; figures come from search excerpts. — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Citizens analysis (research run Dec 26, 2025; reported Dec 29, 2025): across 26 data points in NFL Week 17 moneyline and totals markets, Kalshi pre-game odds were 8% worse than FanDuel and 6% worse than DraftKings **including transaction fees**; methodology not disclosed. — [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/kalshi-pricing-remains-worse-than-sportsbooks-says-citizens/)
- CoinCarp fee comparison (488 games): all-in cost of a $100 even-money bet ≈ $0.95 Novig, $1.60 ProphetX, $3.15 Polymarket, $4.30 Kalshi, $3.10 Pinnacle, $4.90 regulated US book. Methodology not visible. — [CoinCarp](https://www.coincarp.com/article/polymarket-kalshi-novig-or-prophetx-which-prediction-market-is-really-the-cheapest-place-to-bet-on-sports/)
- OddsPapi (vendor) NFL opener: Polymarket moneyline margin 0.98%, Kalshi 1.02%, Pinnacle 3.22% — but Polymarket's best price had only $7.22 of stake at top of book. — [OddsPapi blog](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3660)
- OddsPapi (vendor, Mar 16, 2026) "Backtest Polymarket vs Sportsbooks" reports **no** numbers; only the qualitative line "Based on early data, Pinnacle tends to be better calibrated on sports events." — [OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2489)
- OddsPapi (vendor) asserts, without measurement, that Polymarket and sportsbook odds diverge 3-8% on the same events and that Polymarket reprices news more slowly. — [OddsPapi arbitrage post](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2536)
- Who wins on Kalshi: an SSRN study cited by The American Prospect (Aug 26, 2026) found takers lose 1.12% per trade on average and makers gain 1.12% (reversed before institutional LPs arrived); Roosevelt Institute: takers lost $584M from July 2021 launch to mid-May 2026; Sportico: retail lost $117M Jan-Apr 2026 to market makers on Kalshi "combos"; Kalshi's first market-maker partnership (Susquehanna) began April 2024; DraftKings says pro/institutional traders are 80-90% of its prediction-market volume. — [The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)
- Craig (2025) study of 86M Polymarket bets Apr 2023-Apr 2024: arbitrageurs captured ~$40M; sports markets had the most frequent (intra-Polymarket, sum<100%) arbitrage opportunities; most arb trades returned 1-5%. — [Stafforini summary of Craig 2025](https://stafforini.com/works/craig-2025-polymarket-users-lost/)

**Practitioner evidence of running "devigged sportsbook vs Polymarket" live**
- polymm (open source, MIT, retired): quoted 1 cent above best bid on Polymarket sports/esports when devigged bookmaker fair value exceeded price by `min_edge` (default 5%), hedged the other side; residual unhedged positions (entered at 7%+ edge) became directional. Ran "a few months in early 2026": arb leg +$8,293, directional residual **-$3,184**, net ≈ +$5k, all on-chain (wallet @b00k13). Odds source in the public repo is the-odds-api ("public, paid API"); the author's private scraping pipeline is not included. Retired because it "got too slow to defend its edge"; author warns "Run it as-is and you'll donate to quicker bots" and that "the edge was fresh odds and speed." An adverse-selection post-mortem was published June 30, 2026 (not retrievable; pm.wiki redirects to X). — [GitHub kachence/polymm](https://github.com/kachence/polymm)
- A Polymarket "sports edge" OpenClaw skill and "Arbibot" skill exist that compare sportsbook consensus to Polymarket/Kalshi price; no verified track records. — [ClawKit](https://getclawkit.com/skills/official-0xjims-polymarket-sports-edge)
- Odds-API.io (vendor) says prediction-market arbitrage windows last "seconds to minutes". — [Odds-API.io](https://odds-api.io/blog/polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-sports-betting-api)

### Inferences
- The only direct live test found (polymm) is consistent with the sharp-line edge on Polymarket being real but (a) small, (b) captured mostly by maker/hedge mechanics rather than directional bets, and (c) latency-bound — slower bots get adversely selected. The author's own split (arb +$8.3k, directional -$3.2k) is the single most informative data point for a "devigged Pinnacle vs exchange" taker strategy: directional residuals at 7%+ apparent edge still lost.
- Pinnacle's edge against *other sportsbooks* has decayed (realised 3.5% → 1.9% ROI post-2023/24 on football closers), which plausibly also means less free money versus exchanges whose market makers (Susquehanna, Jump, Flutter, Kalshi Trading) now consume the same sharp feeds.
- Blockworks' 0.996 correlation with FanDuel plus Citizens' "worse than FanDuel after fees" together imply Kalshi pre-game prices are essentially sportsbook-derived; a sharp-line-vs-Kalshi strategy must beat ~2.75% round-trip cost (Blockworks) — i.e., only outliers in thin markets are plausibly tradeable.
- Lead time: nothing measured. Simon (2025) shows MLB lines can get *worse* in the final 90 minutes, which argues for evaluating edge at multiple lead times rather than only at close.

### Gaps
- No academic or independent study measuring CLV/ROI of trading Polymarket or Kalshi against Pinnacle/Circa/Betfair fair value, at any lead time. The Blockworks and Citizens analyses are unreviewed industry research with partially undisclosed methodology.
- Could not retrieve the Blockworks full report (404 on both URLs), the Citizens report itself, the polymm adverse-selection post-mortem, or the SSRN taker/maker study (unnamed in the Prospect article).
- No evidence found specific to tennis, MLB, or table tennis markets on Kalshi/Polymarket versus Pinnacle; everything is NFL/NBA/football.
- Circa and Betfair as alternative fair-value anchors vs exchanges: no measurements found.

## Key Question 2: Which odds providers offer historical and live lines with server-usable API access? Cost, coverage (tennis/MLB/table tennis), latency.

### Takeaway
The Odds API is the only provider with fully public pricing (free 500 credits/mo → $30/20k → $59/100k → $119/5M → $249/15M credits; historical odds at 10x credit cost, 5-min snapshots since Sept 2022) and it now carries Pinnacle, Betfair Exchange, Kalshi, Polymarket, Novig and ProphetX — but Pinnacle is scraped from the public website "which may incur a delay." OpticOdds, SportsDataIO and Sportradar are sales-gated (third-party estimates: ~$5k/mo/sport OpticOdds; ~$16.5k/yr median SportsDataIO). Betfair historic data has a free Basic tier and paid Advanced/Pro (prices behind login). Table-tennis odds are not confirmed at any of these; BetsAPI's site blocked fetching.

### Inferences
- For a server-side US operator the realistic stack is The Odds API (public pricing, Pinnacle + exchanges + Kalshi/Polymarket in one call, 5-min historical snapshots for backtests) with the explicit caveat that its Pinnacle feed is a website scrape of unknown delay — fine for pre-match fair value at 10+ minute lead times, not for line-movement/steam detection.
- Historical credit cost (10×) makes The Odds API backtests expensive at scale: one market × one region × 5-min snapshots over a season runs into millions of credits; the $119/5M tier is the practical floor for a multi-season tennis+MLB backtest.
- Enterprise feeds (OpticOdds, Sportradar, SportsDataIO) are priced at $5k-$16k+/yr-to-month levels that exceed plausible paper-mode budgets; their latency claims ("sub-second") are vendor-only.

## Key Question 3: In-play — score/point feeds for tennis and table tennis, latency vs exchange books, evidence in-play models beat exchange prices after latency.

### Takeaway
Official tennis point feeds come from the umpire's chair via Sportradar (ATP) / Stats Perform→Sportradar (WTA), with no published latency; courtsiders beat even the official feed, and tours deliberately delay broadcasts by seconds. An Imperial College paper shows the Betfair tennis market itself reprices fast enough that the score can be inferred *from the market* — the market is the fastest cheap feed, which is the opposite of an exploitable lag. No evidence found that an in-play model beats exchange or prediction-market in-play prices after latency; in-play Kalshi liquidity is thin and both Crypto.com (3 s, makers exempt) and Kalshi (pending CFTC filing) impose order delays on takers.

### Cited Findings
- Academic (Dumitrescu, Huang, Knottenbelt, Spanias, Wozniak, Imperial College): "A problem faced by participants in tennis-related betting exchange markets is the lack of a reliable, low-latency and low-cost point-to-point score feed"; umpire-entered scores "are prone to delays and do not provide the data in an easily retrievable way"; exchange liquidity "is provided by bets placed by both humans and automated trading systems alike... the majority of liquidity is provided by automated systems"; the authors infer score from Match Odds market data sampled "every second or so", using volatility over the most recent 10-second window and an 8-second minimum between inferred points; detection errors still occur and can derail the algorithm. — [Imperial College PDF](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~wjk/publications/DumitrescuHuangKnottenbeltSpaniasWozniak.pdf)
- Courtsiding: transmitting live info from the venue for betting; "observed as occurring most prominently, although not exclusively, in tennis"; targets are often low-profile matches with only internet distribution; Wimbledon 2025 deployed undercover staff and ejected several courtsiders; legal status varies (not an offence in the UK per the Gambling Commission). — [Wikipedia: Courtsiding](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Courtsiding); [Casino.org on Wimbledon 2025](https://www.casino.org/news/wimbledon-unleashed-undercover-agents-to-catch-betting-spies/); [FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/inside-the-shadowy-world-of-high-speed-tennis-betting)
- ATP/WTA keep TV broadcasts on a short delay of a few seconds to blunt betting on the stream; the official feed originates from the umpire's chair. — [ESPN (c. 2012)](https://www.espn.com/chalk/story/_/id/13481104/whatsapp:/send)
- Stats Perform (vendor): most operators run in-play on streams 5-8 seconds behind the event; in tennis over 80% of sportsbook turnover is in-play. — [SBC News, May 20 2026](https://sbcnews.co.uk/latestnews/2026/05/20/cost-stream-delay-live-betting/)
- GVC reported a 66% cut in in-play bet delays after moving to automated feeds (2017). — [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2017/06/21/gvc-optimises-tennis-markets-ahead-wimbledon/)
- UK Gambling Commission: exchanges impose in-play delays "to protect its bettors"; delay length varies by operator, event, and data-source latency. A 2026 operator guide puts typical bet-delay at 1-5 seconds. An unverified forum report cites 5-6 s on Betfair tennis. — [UKGC](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/in-play-or-in-running-betting); [track360 (vendor)](https://track360.io/blog/live-in-play-betting-trading-software-stack-operator-guide-2026); [SBR forum (anecdote)](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/sportsbooks-industry/1897775-betfair-the-tennis-match-a-kalinskaya-vs-s-rogers)
- Crypto.com prediction market: ~3-second delay on sports wagers for some customers; market makers exempt, letting them reprice before fast takers land. Kalshi filed a proposal with the CFTC (reported Dec 9, 2025) for its own order-execution delays, customers covered unspecified, 10-business-day review; no confirmation of final length found. — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/securities-law/wall-street-gets-a-3-second-head-start-over-retail-in-event-bets); [Gambling911](https://www.gambling911.com/node/78397)
- Blockworks: Kalshi in-game liquidity is thin and arbitrage gaps versus FanDuel are short-lived. — [Blockworks](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)
- Table tennis in-play: Setka Cup is BETER's own 24/7 in-house product with "live streaming, real-time data, hyper-accurate odds" from BETER's in-house traders; distributed via FeedConstruct's odds aggregation platform (Jan 2025; >11,000 events/month incl. BSKT Cup). No independent score feed or latency figure. "TT Elite" did not appear in any result. — [iGaming Future](https://igamingfuture.com/beter-expands-sports-portfolio-with-live-streaming-and-odds-from-official-national-table-tennis-tournaments/); [European Gaming](https://europeangaming.eu/portal/latest-news/2025/01/15/174041/beter-and-feedconstruct-unite-in-a-major-content-deal/)

### Inferences
- Because liquid exchange tennis markets reprice within seconds of a point and are dominated by automated systems with faster (or courtside) feeds, a model fed by any commercially available score API (Sportradar/Enetpulse/flashscore-style) will typically be *behind* the book, not ahead; the Imperial paper's approach (infer state from the market) is the pragmatic design, and it concedes errors.
- Taker-side order delays on prediction markets (Crypto.com 3 s; Kalshi pending) structurally hand in-play edge to exempt market makers, so an in-play taker strategy on Kalshi/Polymarket US faces both feed latency and an execution delay.

### Gaps
- No published latency numbers for Sportradar ATP, Stats Perform WTA, Enetpulse, or any flashscore-style scrape; no study measuring in-play model profitability net of latency on Betfair, Kalshi, or Polymarket.
- Polymarket US in-game delay policy and Kalshi's final delay length not found.
- No legitimate independent point feed for Setka Cup/TT Elite/Liga Pro identified.

## Key Question 4: MLB information edges — lineup/starting-pitcher confirmation timing, umpires, weather/wind, bullpen; who sells these and evidence of value vs market.

### Takeaway
Since 2019 MLB routes lineups and umpire assignments through the commissioner's office to an official feed (Sportradar/Stats Perform/Perform as licensed distributors) with a reported ~15-minute confidential hold, after which gaming partners and media receive them — so the information edge belongs to feed licensees, not scrapers. Lineups typically post ~2 hours before first pitch (late scratches 30-60 min); umpire crews 2-3 hours before, with series openers unknown. No rigorous study quantifying betting value of lineups/umpires/weather versus the closing line was found; Simon (2025) shows MLB lines are non-monotonically efficient in the final 90 minutes, which is the only academic hook.

### Cited Findings
- Lineup cards are posted "approximately 2 hours before game time"; teams may hold a questionable player and lock lineups 30-60 min before first pitch. — [FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/mlb/lineups/)
- Umpire crews "usually get announced 2-3 hours before first pitch"; for series openers assignments are mostly unknown, thereafter the plate rotates through the crew (next plate umpire is typically the prior game's first-base umpire). — [Fantasy Info Central](https://www.fantasyinfocentral.com/mlb/lineups)
