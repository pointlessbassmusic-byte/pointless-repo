# Execution, Not Forecasting, Survives Prediction-Market Fees

No public-data forecasting model for tennis or MLB has demonstrably beaten a sharp closing price after fees on a large out-of-sample set, and the four things that *have* measurably beaten market prices are all structural rather than predictive: using a sharper venue's de-vigged line as fair value against a softer one (**+1.8% to +3.7% realised ROI over 1,900–31,000 bets, decaying to +1.9% since 2023/24**), running a deliberately *decorrelated* model with portfolio staking (**+1.5–1.7 units per NBA season against Pinnacle closing after margin**, with lower accuracy than the bookmaker), passive liquidity provision held to settlement (**+0.82–1.91¢ per contract gross of fees across 41.6M Kalshi trades**; **+2.29% on $35.9M of pair assembly** for the largest Polymarket sports wallet), and a thin favourite-side carry (**Kalshi makers above 50¢ earn +2.6% after taker fees with a 33% standard deviation**). Every other candidate — favourite-longshot bias in sports, Binance-to-Polymarket lead-lag, in-play tennis latency, cross-venue gaps, complete-set arbitrage after the 2026 fee rollout — either loses after fees, is unmeasured, or is inverted in the one category that matters to this bot (Polymarket sports longshots earn **+18% pooled**, the opposite of the sportsbook prior). The academic benchmarks are unambiguous: Vegas closing beat FiveThirtyEight's Elo-plus-pitcher MLB model over **25,165 games** with a confidence sequence excluding zero, and Pinnacle beats every tennis rating model by 0.5–3 accuracy points. That matches what this bot measured on its own tape (MLB model-vs-market beta 0.065, t = 0.20, n = 873; tennis maker mark-outs negative at every horizon over 34,454 fills). The implication is to stop spending effort on rating features and restructure around three measurable things: a sharp-line CLV harness as the *only* evaluation metric, maker-side-only execution graded hold-to-settlement rather than by short mark-outs, and a decorrelation objective for whatever model survives — while shutting down table tennis, crypto windows, drift, FLB and taker-side arbitrage, none of which has evidence behind it.

## Sharp closing lines beat every public tennis and MLB model

The tennis literature has one stable pattern: rating models land just behind the bookmaker on every large sample and tie only on small, favourite-heavy Grand Slam samples. Kovalchik's test of 11 published forecasters on **2,395 ATP matches** ranked FiveThirtyEight-style Elo second behind market consensus at roughly 70% accuracy, with all models 10–20 points worse on lower-ranked players ([JQAS 10.1515/jqas-2015-0059](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2015-0059/html)). A 2022–2025 graph-neural-network study found Pinnacle implied probability at **68.7% accuracy and Brier 0.198 versus weighted Elo at 65.8% and 0.214** ([arXiv 2510.20454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20454)); a 2025 dynamic-graph model on 1,686 major-tournament matches landed at 73.5% against the bookmakers' 74.1% with p = 0.40 for the difference ([arXiv 2508.15956](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15956)); and Wilkens' 2010–2019 ATP/WTA sweep of logistic regression, random forests, SVMs, ANNs and GBMs concluded that "most of the relevant information is embedded in the betting markets" and that returns were "mainly negative over the longer term" across every staking scheme ([JSA-200463](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-200463)). The one small sample where a model tied the market — Sackmann's 117-match 2016 US Open comparison, Pinnacle Brier .172 against FiveThirtyEight Elo .171 — is also the one where Pinnacle was underconfident in its 60–69% bin ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/?p=2129)), which is a hint about *where* residual error lives (mid-range favourites) rather than evidence of a beatable market.

The positive-ROI tennis claims do not survive inspection. Sipko's ANN with Kelly staking reported **+4.35% ROI on 3,183 bets** drawn from 6,315 ATP matches in 2013–2014 with Pinnacle pre-match odds, but only after discarding the 50% most uncertain matches, with no significance test, odds timing unspecified, and the author's own note that ROI "was a very unstable evaluation metric" and that the ROI-optimised feature set that hit 11.3% on validation did not generalise ([Sipko thesis](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/teaching/distinguished-projects/2015/m.sipko.pdf)). Lisi's 15.9% came from **501 Grand Slam matches** in a single year using the odds themselves as a model input ([EJASA](https://ese-journals.unisalento.it/index.php/ejasa/article/view/16516)). The decisive methodological result is Lyócsa & Výrost: forty betting rules that looked individually profitable lost their significance once data-snooping bias was controlled, leaving the tennis market "much more efficient than suggested by previous studies" ([Applied Economics 50(20)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v50y2018i20p2251-2272.html)). Wunderlich & Memmert formalise why: positive betting returns can arise "either consistently or by chance even when a model is not more accurate than alternatives," so returns from a single window are not evidence of accuracy ([IJF 36(2)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v36y2020i2p713-722.html)). The best-known public tennis repo, edouardthom/ATPBetting (467 stars, abandoned 2021), reports 58–70% ROI from the top-confidence decile of 11,054 matches after tuning hyperparameters on ROI, against a random-betting baseline that sums the winner's odds over a random half of matches with no fixed seed; it is a feature-engineering reference, not evidence ([ATPBetting notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/edouardthom/ATPBetting/master/Beating%20the%20bookmakers%20on%20tennis%20matches.ipynb)).

MLB is cleaner because one study has real power. Choe & Ramdas compared forecasters on every regular-season and postseason game from 2010 to 2019, **T = 25,165**, with anytime-valid confidence sequences: the 95% empirical-Bernstein sequence for the Brier differential of FiveThirtyEight's Elo-with-starting-pitcher model minus Vegas closing odds was **(−0.00265, −0.00062)**, with an e-value of 2,979 against the null that 538 was at least as good; "none of the other forecasters, including 538, have outperformed vegas from 2010 to 2019" ([arXiv 2110.00115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00115)). The edge is tiny — about 0.002 Brier — but it is the wrong sign for a model that then has to clear a 2–4.5% moneyline vig, and nothing in the systematic review of eight MLB machine-learning studies reports a single ROI figure; only one (70 games, Bet365) even uses a bookmaker benchmark ([arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21484v1)). The one public MLB repo with a market-aware design, mmoore07129/mlb-kalshi-bot, ran its own CLV backtest on 2024–2025 and found its 53-feature XGBoost model **anti-calibrated on disagreement games, with a Pinnacle-led blend winning about 77% of head-to-heads at gaps of 20 points or more**; the author relegated the model to a fallback used only when Pinnacle posts no line ([mlb-kalshi-bot](https://github.com/mmoore07129/mlb-kalshi-bot)). That is exactly the result this bot's own harness produced on 873 Kalshi MLB games — market Brier 0.2399 versus model 0.2422, blend worse than market alone, regression of market error on model disagreement at beta 0.065 with t = 0.20 (`docs/EDGE_VERDICT_2026-09-23.md`). The literature and the bot agree; there is nothing left to find in Elo-family ratings against a sharp line.

One reframing from the reviews matters for what to keep. Hubáček & Šír showed on NBA data that profit does not require beating the market on accuracy, only being *decorrelated* from it where it errs: a neural model with 67.15% accuracy against the bookmaker's 69% earned **+1.74 ± 0.14 units per season (walk-forward, 2006–2014, Pinnacle closing odds with 2.5% margin embedded)** when trained with a decorrelation weight of γ = 0.4 and staked through a portfolio rule, versus +0.38 at γ = 0 and −1.92 at γ = 1; uniform staking was negative at every γ ([arXiv 2010.12508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508)). Walsh & Joshi report 69.86% higher average returns from selecting an NBA model on calibration rather than accuracy ([Walsh & Joshi](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/machine-learning-for-sports-betting-should-model-selection-be-bas/)). Neither is tennis or MLB, and the Hubáček edge is small and strategy-dependent, but it is the only academic recipe for a model-versus-market bot that comes with a fee-inclusive, walk-forward number.

Three things stand out. First, the strongest fee-inclusive edges are *relative-price* edges: a sharp venue's line applied to a softer one. Data Golf's expected-versus-realised ROI was roughly 1:1 when Pinnacle's close was the fair value but only weakly positive using DraftKings' close, and random betting at margin-free Pinnacle odds over 5,000 bets spans roughly −5.9% to −1.6% at the 5th–95th percentiles, so 2–3% deviations are noise even at 10,000 bets ([Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/analyzing-betting-odds)). The decay from 3.5% to 1.9% realised in football since 2023/24 ([Networked](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)) is the most important trend line in this report: the same sharp feeds now flow into Susquehanna, Kalshi Trading and the other institutional makers on the venues this bot trades ([Bartlett & O'Hara](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)). Second, in every live attempt at "sharp line versus prediction market," the directional (taker) residual lost and only the hedged or maker leg made money: polymm's split, RN1's −2.05% directional against +2.29% pair assembly, crollila's CI spanning zero. Third, the two honest open-source forward tests (ryanfrigo, crollila) are statistically null after 55 and 232 positions, and the fill-model author estimated that **~1,400 resolutions** are needed just to distinguish +4.5% from −2.05% ([polymarket-fill-model](https://github.com/sacha9214/polymarket-fill-model)) — which sets the minimum sample for any experiment below.

No study measures Polymarket or Kalshi sports prices against Pinnacle's close at any lead time; the notes are explicit that this is the single most relevant gap for this repo. What exists is circumstantial: Kalshi pre-game prices track FanDuel at correlations up to 0.996 with a 2.75% cost threshold leaving few short-lived arbitrage intervals ([Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets)), and a Citizens analysis of 26 NFL Week 17 data points found Kalshi pre-game odds **8% worse than FanDuel and 6% worse than DraftKings including fees** ([CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/kalshi-pricing-remains-worse-than-sportsbooks-says-citizens/)). Both are industry research with undisclosed methodology, and neither covers tennis or MLB. The claim that sportsbook consensus *leads* Polymarket is a vendor assertion with no timestamps ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2536)); the one rigorous lead-lag paper says Kalshi leads *sports* when it has higher relative volume while Polymarket leads politics ([SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)), which is why the constraint against taking the lagging side of a Kalshi-to-Polymarket move is also the evidence-consistent choice.

## Sports moneylines are calibrated; the profits go to whoever rests orders

The venue-specific evidence removes the biases this bot already tested. The favourite-longshot bias is real platform-wide on Polymarket — sub-10¢ purchases lose 19.35¢ per dollar pooled — but the two-sided pattern is "robust in Crypto and Politics but surprisingly absent in Sports," where longshots earn +2.43% equal-weighted and +18.11% pooled and favourites earn −0.23% / +0.137% ([arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12878v1)). On Kalshi the bias is present in every category and every volume quintile, but the 2025 coefficient is "smaller and less statistically significant," and sports were only added in January 2025 ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Twenty-three million Kalshi moneyline trades show calibration parameters at perfect-calibration reference values mid-life, departing sharply only in the **final ten minutes before expiry**, where the curve becomes step-like from insurance buying by traders holding losing positions, and cross-game parlays priced above the product of their legs ([arXiv 2607.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14430)). Across 353M trades on both venues, sports prices "track nearer the diagonal" than politics ([arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)). So the pre-match sports moneyline — the only thing this bot trades — is the *best*-calibrated product on either venue, and the documented miscalibrations are in-play, near-expiry or derivative.

Who earns is equally clear. On the Polymarket dataset of 588M trades, the top 1% of users capture 76.5% of profits, successful traders provide liquidity with limit orders while unsuccessful ones take, and **sports accounted for 81% of top-user gains** ([SSRN 6443103 via HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48221877)). Della Vedova's 222M-trade decomposition finds forecasting and execution skill share under 1% of variance; automated traders with **49.9% directional accuracy made ≈$133M in aggregate** while retail with 51.3% accuracy lost, with about 70% of bot edge from timing and the rest from spread capture ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/who-profits-from-prediction-markets/)). Takers on Kalshi lose 1.12% per trade on average and makers gain the mirror image, with takers having lost $584M from launch to mid-May 2026 ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)). None of this is a forecasting result; it is the fee-and-spread arithmetic of a binary order book.

The maker economics are positive but thin and concentrated. Bartlett & O'Hara's mechanism matters more than the headline: makers earn not from spread capture but from *underwriting* a YES-overbetting surplus — maker win rate 63.4% against 53.8% predicted under independence — which cross-subsidises informed flow whose price impact is "almost entirely permanent"; losses are concentrated when trailing one-sided flow (VPIN) exceeds about 0.86–0.94 on single-name markets, where buckets lose $68 with a 59.4% loss rate, while on broad-based markets VPIN has no predictive power ([Bartlett & O'Hara](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f243677.pdf)). The maker's realised round trip is hold-to-settlement: 92% of single-event positions and 95% of crypto gross volume are carried to resolution. On Polymarket, Dubach's 600-market panel finds median quoted spreads around **400 bps** at mid prices with a Glosten-Harris adverse-selection component of 0.0 probability points in the top-100 markets, but also that feed-inferred trade direction matches on-chain truth only 59% of the time, enough to flip the sign of measured half-spreads in half to two-thirds of markets ([Dubach](https://philippdubach.com/posts/the-anatomy-of-a-decentralized-prediction-market-notes-from-the-polymarket-order-book/)). The paper-trading fill model on Polymarket sports O/U measured a **+1.13¢ one-minute mark-out with post-fill drift of +0.13¢**, but its daily P&L swung $35 between conservative and optimistic queue assumptions (−$6.26 vs +$28.51), a 5-share clip sat about 120th in a 600–800-share best-bid queue, and 495 of 673 two-sided markets printed no trade in 899 market-hours ([polymarket-fill-model](https://github.com/sacha9214/polymarket-fill-model)). Profit is concentrated the same way as elsewhere: 0.55% of profitable maker wallets took half the maker gains in Dec 2025–Feb 2026 political markets ([coinalertnews](https://coinalertnews.com/news/2026/04/29/polymarket-profit-concentration-inequality)).

This is where the bot's own negative result needs reconciling rather than accepting. The repo's mark-out study on 105 Kalshi tennis markets found maker fills **84/16 skewed toward selling YES**, mean mark-outs of −0.00104 at 60 s worsening to −0.00645 at 1,800 s, and a median of exactly zero (`docs/MARKOUT_2026-09-23.md`). Bartlett & O'Hara's data show the same thing — takers overwhelmingly buy YES — and conclude the maker's edge is *in that imbalance*, realised at settlement, not in short-horizon mark-outs. A maker who is short YES against YES-buyers who overpay wins 63% of the time at settlement even though the price drifts against her for the next half hour. The bot measured the drift and called it adverse selection; the only measurement that can decide whether the Kalshi tennis flow is the profitable "behavioral surplus" kind or the toxic informed kind is per-fill P&L held to settlement, split by trailing VPIN, with the Kalshi maker fee applied. That is a replay of data the repo already has.

Fees decide what a small account can rest. Polymarket's global CLOB charges takers 0.05·p(1−p) on sports and rebates makers 15% of that — **≈0.19¢ per share filled at mid, roughly $1.90/day gross for $1,000 of daily maker fills**, essentially the $1 daily minimum payout ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/maker-rebates-program)). Its sports liquidity-reward pools were time-boxed campaigns (April 2026; World Cup June 11–July 19) and the methodology page lists no active sports program ([Polymarket docs: Liquidity Rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/liquidity-rewards)); it is also geoblocked for US IPs, so for this bot it remains a paper venue ([Polymarket geoblock docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock)). Kalshi charges makers 0.0175·p(1−p) **rounded up to the next cent** on designated series including major sports games, so a resting order at 50¢ pays 0.4375¢ that rounds to a full cent per contract on small clips — the entire one-tick spread — and its open Liquidity Incentive Program requires resting size on *both* sides against 100–20,000-contract targets sampled once per second ([sailgp quoting the Kalshi schedule](https://sailgp.com/prediction-markets/kalshi/fees); [CFTC LIP filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf)). Series with `fee_type = quadratic` charge no maker fee ([Allium Kalshi series docs](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series)). Polymarket US (QCX) is the one venue with a per-fill maker rebate paid regardless of pool share — Sacra reports **taker 0.05, maker rebate −0.0125** capped at $1.25 per 100 contracts, and a July 2026 CFTC notice raised the taker coefficient to 0.06 ([Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/polymarket/); [CFTC rules0707269057](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules0707269057.pdf)) — but it is iOS-app and KYC gated and no programmatic quoting API was found in any source.

## Which data is worth buying and which feeds are dead ends

The only input with measured value against market prices is a sharp de-vigged line, and the only US-usable source with public pricing is The Odds API: free 500 credits/month, **$30 for 20k, $59 for 100k, $119 for 5M**, with historical snapshots at 5-minute intervals since September 2022 costing 10× credits, and a bookmaker list that now includes Pinnacle (scraped from the public website "which may incur a delay"), Betfair Exchange, Kalshi, Polymarket, Novig and ProphetX in one call; tennis is keyed per tournament and table tennis is not listed ([the-odds-api.com](https://the-odds-api.com/); [API docs v4](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/); [bookmaker list](https://the-odds-api.com/sports-odds-data/bookmaker-apis.html)). Its terms allow commercial derived use and indefinite storage but bar redistribution and disclaim accuracy ([Terms](https://the-odds-api.com/terms-and-conditions.html)). Pinnacle closed its public API in July 2025, so every "Pinnacle feed" is now a scrape ([dev.to](https://dev.to/ryankr/pinnacle-killed-its-public-api-heres-how-to-get-pinnacle-odds-in-2026-with-code-39pe)); that is adequate for pre-match fair value at 10-minute-plus leads and inadequate for steam detection. Enterprise feeds are out of a paper-mode budget: OpticOdds reportedly ~$5,000/month per sport and SportsDataIO a median $16,496/year, neither with public pricing or independent latency numbers ([Odds-API.io comparison](https://odds-api.io/compare/optic-odds); [Vendr](https://vendr.com/marketplace/sportsdataio)). Free historical pre-match Pinnacle and Bet365 columns for tennis back to 2000 exist in the tennis-data.co.uk yearly files that ATPBetting and the R package `welo` load ([welo manual](https://vincenzocandila.r-universe.dev/welo/doc/manual.html)), and Shin de-vigging — which Štrumbelj showed predicts better than basic normalisation — is a pip install ([shin](https://pypi.org/project/shin)). The Sackmann archive stays CC BY-NC-SA, which also binds derived datasets ([Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Aneeshers/tennis-sackmann-archive)).

In-play feeds are a dead end for this bot. Imperial College's tennis work states the problem directly — "the lack of a reliable, low-latency and low-cost point-to-point score feed" — and solves it by inferring the score *from the Betfair market itself* sampled every second, because exchange liquidity is dominated by automated systems with faster or courtside information ([Imperial College PDF](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~wjk/publications/DumitrescuHuangKnottenbeltSpaniasWozniak.pdf)). Operators run in-play 5–8 seconds behind the event ([SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/latestnews/2026/05/20/cost-stream-delay-live-betting/)), Crypto.com gives makers a 3-second head start over takers and Kalshi has filed for its own taker delay ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/securities-law/wall-street-gets-a-3-second-head-start-over-retail-in-event-bets)), and the only academic in-play tennis inefficiency — prices under-reacting to a conceded service break — is in a paper whose full text could not be retrieved ([Newcastle repository](https://ogma.newcastle.edu.au/vital/access/manager/Repository/uon:9686)). The same holds for MLB information: lineups and umpire assignments route through the commissioner's office with a reported 15-minute hold before release to licensed feeds ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/30038/mlb-lineup-weaponize-official-data/)), so a scraper is at best simultaneous with public release, and no study quantifies lineup, umpire or weather value against the closing line. The one MLB-specific hook is Simon's Management Science result on 3,681 games: lines overreact with significantly negatively autocorrelated open-to-close changes, weekend day-game forecasts were *worse* at first pitch than 90 minutes earlier, and weak-form efficiency is rejected ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.00456)); Bouchard's 88,000-game thesis found larger relative open-to-close moves are on average less accurate ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/434dc6e1-ce30-401a-9a71-4fd161085ec9)). Neither reports a strategy return, but both are testable from The Odds API's historical snapshots.

Table tennis has no legitimate data path and a documented integrity problem. Setka Cup is BETER's own in-house product, priced by BETER's own traders and distributed through an aggregator ([iGaming Future](https://igamingfuture.com/beter-expands-sports-portfolio-with-live-streaming-and-odds-from-official-national-table-tennis-tournaments/)), so any "sharp line" for it is circular. New Jersey and Colorado suspended Ukrainian table-tennis wagering in 2020 and Indiana banned all non-ITTF/ETTU events ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/general/news/new-jersey-suspends-ukrainian-table-tennis-betting-over-match-fixing-concerns)); **all seven IBIA table-tennis alerts in Q1 2026 were in non-ITTF events** ([IBIA Q1 2026 PDF](https://ibia.bet/media/documents/Q12026FINAL.pdf)); no public repo models the fast leagues at all; and the repo's own measurement found Setka Cup on Polymarket does about $34 a day across 100 events (`docs/EDGE_VERDICT_2026-09-23.md`). Crypto up/down windows are worse than unmeasured: the one rigorous Binance-to-Polymarket model lost −0.116 units per trade after the 0.07 crypto taker fee ([arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26245)), the 500 ms taker delay that made maker quoting on 5-minute BTC "basically free money" was removed in February 2026 ([Protos](https://protos.com/polymarket-ends-trading-loophole-for-bitcoin-quants/)), and 821 wallets earned ~$8.2M pushing settlement prints, flipping 34% of outcomes priced 90–100% ([arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31675v1)). Cross-venue gaps are a structural 2–4% that persists because of resolution-rule differences, not information ([arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706)) — about one Kalshi round trip plus mis-resolution risk — and the complete-set opportunities that paid $39.6M were measured at zero fees, with 2–5¢ thresholds now largely below the 1.0–1.75% mid-price fee on every category except geopolitics ([arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1)).

Of the roughly thirty public bots surveyed, none supplies a tennis, MLB or table-tennis edge, and star counts are inverse to honesty: the 2,903-star CloddsBot carries a Solana token address and paid services, the 589-star BTC bot has four commits and a placeholder learning engine, while the two repos with confidence intervals have 0 and 612 stars ([CloddsBot](https://github.com/alsk1992/CloddsBot); [aulekator](https://github.com/aulekator/Polymarket-BTC-15-Minute-Trading-Bot)). The transferable pieces are techniques: mlb-kalshi-bot's 5-minute CLV snapshotter that records the last sharp-book price before first pitch as the close and reads fees from Kalshi's `/portfolio/settlements` ([mlb-kalshi-bot](https://github.com/mmoore07129/mlb-kalshi-bot)); crollila's post-mortem discipline of reporting ROI with a CI and the share of profit from the top two positions ([polymarket-vegas-edge](https://github.com/crollila/polymarket-vegas-edge)); ryanfrigo's "Edge Policy" that disables a category after settled-trade evidence ([kalshi-ai-trading-bot](https://github.com/ryanfrigo/kalshi-ai-trading-bot)); poly-maker's regime machine that pulls quotes on sweeps and halts on stale WebSocket data, with no backtester and an explicit warning that it can lose money ([poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)); and `welo`'s bootstrap-CI ROI with a random-side benchmark, about 100 lines to port ([welo manual](https://vincenzocandila.r-universe.dev/welo/doc/manual.html)). The official SDK landscape also moved: `py-clob-client` is archived and non-functional, `polymarket-client` (Polymarket/py-sdk) is the replacement at 0.x semver, and Kalshi's docs now point to `kalshi_python_sync`/`kalshi_python_async` ([py-sdk](https://github.com/Polymarket/py-sdk); [Kalshi python-sdk docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/python-sdk)).

## Restructure around measurement, maker-side execution and decorrelation

The ranking below orders changes by the strength of the evidence behind them, states what decides each, and keeps every constraint: Kalshi and Polymarket US are the only live-capable venues, Polymarket's global CLOB stays paper, paper is the default, risk fails closed, nothing ever adds to a loser, and nothing takes the lagging side of a Kalshi-to-Polymarket move.

| Rank | Action | Evidence grade | Decides it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Build** a sharp-line CLV harness and make it the only evaluation metric | A (Data Golf, Networked; mlb-kalshi-bot) | ≥1,000 paper decisions graded against Shin-de-vigged Pinnacle close at ≥10 min lead; pass = mean CLV exceeds the fee at that price with a bootstrap CI excluding zero |
| 2 | **Build** maker-only sharp-line quoting on Kalshi (paper) | A- (polymm split, RN1, crollila) | ≥1,400 resolutions; pass = hold-to-settlement P&L net of Kalshi maker fee > 0 with CI excluding zero *and* CLV ≥ 0; directional residuals accounted separately |
| 3 | **Re-run** the maker mark-out study as hold-to-settlement P&L split by VPIN | B (Bartlett & O'Hara) | Replay of existing Kalshi tennis tape; pass = per-fill settlement P&L > rounded maker fee in VPIN < 0.86 buckets; fail kills passive quoting for good |
| 4 | **Modify** the surviving model's objective from accuracy to decorrelation | A for NBA only (Hubáček & Šír) | Beta of (outcome − market) on (model − market) > 0 with t > 2 over ≥2,000 games; otherwise demote the model to a monitor |
| 5 | **Test** fading large MLB open-to-close moves | descriptive only (Simon, Bouchard) | Historical Odds API snapshots, one pre-registered rule, one season; pass = positive CLV with CI excluding zero |
| 6 | **Buy** The Odds API at $59–119/month; nothing else | — | Needed for 1, 2 and 5; historical credits at 10× make the $119/5M tier the floor for a multi-season backtest |
| 7 | **Stop** table tennis, crypto windows, drift, FLB, taker-side cross-venue and complete-set arbitrage, in-play work | A against each | No experiment; evidence is already negative or the data path is illegitimate |

The first change is cheap and overdue. Every edge that has held up was measured as CLV against a sharp close, and the bot's own harness already identified in-play contamination of Kalshi closes and the home-side drift that fakes positive 24-hour CLV (`sportsbot/backtest/vs_market.py`; `docs/EDGE_VERDICT_2026-09-23.md`). What is missing is an external sharp anchor. Snapshot Pinnacle, Betfair Exchange, Kalshi and Polymarket from The Odds API every five minutes from the morning of each tennis and MLB slate to first pitch or first serve, de-vig Pinnacle with Shin, store the last pre-start Pinnacle price as the close, and grade *every* scanner decision — taken or skipped — against it, in probability space and after the fee at that price. The pass criterion is the Data Golf pattern: expected CLV and realised ROI should move 1:1, and the sample needs at least the 1,000 bets at which random Pinnacle-odds betting still spans several points of ROI ([Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/analyzing-betting-odds)). Reuse `bot/positions.py`'s tighten-only CLV feedback as the enforcement: a sport whose rolling CLV against Pinnacle is negative raises its own bar and cuts its own cap, which is already the loss-only-reduces-risk shape the repo mandates.

The second change is the one strategy with live evidence of positive after-fee P&L against prediction-market prices, restricted to the leg that actually paid. In polymm the hedged maker leg earned +$8,293 while the directional leg at 7%+ apparent edge lost $3,184; in RN1's tape the pair-assembly leg earned +2.29% while directional residuals lost 2.05% with t = −6.32. So rest bids on Kalshi at the Shin-de-vigged Pinnacle price minus the required edge, never lift an offer, and treat any fill that is not hedged by the complementary side within the TTL as a directional position subject to the normal exit and drawdown rules, never as inventory to add to. This is consistent by construction with the no-taker-on-lag constraint and with Blockworks' 2.75% round-trip threshold that makes taking unprofitable. Use Kalshi series with `fee_type = quadratic` first, because the 0.0175 coefficient rounded up to a cent consumes a one-tick spread on small clips on the designated sports series; test whether tennis and MLB game series are designated before quoting them ([Allium series fee docs](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series-fee-changes)). Keep the existing conservative queue model from `maker/tennis_maker_bot.py` — cancels never help, repricing resets the queue — because the fill-model author's $35/day swing between fill assumptions is the single largest source of false positives in this class of experiment. The deciding sample is 1,400 resolutions, which at this bot's slate size is a full season, and the pass condition is net-of-fee settlement P&L *and* non-negative CLV, not either alone, because CLV without P&L is drift and P&L without CLV is luck ([Whelan on CLV](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)).

The third change costs nothing but a replay. The repo concluded "passive quoting does not pay" from mean mark-outs of −0.1 to −0.6¢ at 60–1,800 seconds on 34,454 fills with an 84/16 YES-sell imbalance. Bartlett & O'Hara's 41.6M-trade result says that imbalance is *the* source of maker profit when carried to settlement — the maker sells YES to overpaying YES-buyers and wins 63% of the time — and that losses concentrate in trailing-VPIN buckets above 0.86 on single-event markets. Those are different measurements of the same tape, and only one of them is the maker's real round trip. Re-score every tape-defined pre-match fill in the existing Kalshi tennis sample at settlement, net of the rounded maker fee, bucketed by trailing 20-bucket VPIN, and report mean, median and the share of P&L from the top 2% of fills. If settlement P&L is negative even below VPIN 0.86, passive quoting on Kalshi tennis is dead and rank 2 should be re-scoped to MLB or abandoned; if it is positive there and negative above, the VPIN threshold becomes a quote-pull rule in `bot/risk.py` alongside the existing kill switches — a fail-closed addition, since it only ever removes quotes.

The fourth change is what to do with the models. They lose to the line on accuracy, and the literature says they will keep losing. Hubáček & Šír's recipe is the only fee-inclusive one for keeping a model in a trading path: train it to be *uncorrelated* with the market's error rather than accurate, and stake through a portfolio rule rather than per-bet Kelly. The repo already computes the diagnostic — the regression of market error on model disagreement, beta 0.065 at n = 873 — so the experiment is to add a decorrelation term (γ ≈ 0.4) to the tennis and MLB fits and re-run the same regression on at least 2,000 games. The honest prior is failure: NBA box scores carry far more team-level information than tennis Elo, and the result is "small and strategy-dependent" even there. If beta does not clear t = 2, the model leaves the trading path and becomes a monitor that flags markets where Pinnacle and the venue disagree by more than the fee — which is the only job the mlb-kalshi-bot author found for theirs.

The fifth is a bounded test of the one MLB-specific inefficiency with academic support, and it should be pre-registered the way `maker/l2_replay.py` already pre-registers its grid: one rule (fade open-to-close moves larger than a fixed threshold at a fixed lead), one season of historical Odds API snapshots, one run, graded by CLV against the Pinnacle close. Simon's negative autocorrelation and Bouchard's "big moves are less accurate" are descriptive, not strategy returns, so this ranks below everything with a realised number.

Everything else stops. Table tennis has no independent fair value, a live integrity record, $34 a day of volume, and a model the repo's own docstring says shows no edge; it should be removed from `sports:` rather than left at a higher `min_edge_override`. Crypto windows, drift, FLB and complete-set arbitrage each have a measured null or a measured loss after the 2026 fee rollout, and the FLB prior is inverted in Polymarket sports. Cross-venue "edges" above a few percent are suspect matches, as the arb-scanner's own `max_net_edge` rule already encodes, and the only way to capture the 2–4% structural gap is to hold both legs through two different resolution regimes, which the no-taker-on-lag constraint and the UMA dispute record (over 1,150 arbitrated markets in 2026, with a stakeholder voting in about one dispute in five) both argue against ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/polymarkets-arbitration-model-faces-conflict-of-interest-questions/)). In-play tennis and MLB information edges belong to feed licensees and courtsiders by construction. Nothing should be bought beyond The Odds API; OpticOdds, Sportradar and SportsDataIO are priced for operators and offer no measured advantage at the 10-minute leads this bot can defend.

## Conclusion

The question this bot has been asking — can a rating model out-forecast the price? — has a settled answer in every sport with a large sample, and the answer did not change when the venue switched from Pinnacle to Kalshi or Polymarket, because those venues' sports prices are now sportsbook-derived to three decimal places and quoted by the same institutions that consume the sharp feeds. What the evidence rewards instead is a bot that knows the sharp price better than the venue's takers do, posts on the side of the book that is paid rather than charged, grades itself only against that sharp close, and treats any unhedged position as the losing leg it has repeatedly been shown to be. Even that is a thin business: the measured maker edges are a cent or two per contract before fees, the realised sharp-line edge against soft venues has halved since 2023, and the honest forward tests need four-figure samples to separate a small gain from a small loss.

The restructuring therefore turns the repo from a predictor into a measurement instrument with a narrow execution arm. Its chances of making money are modest; its chances of *knowing* whether it makes money, within a season, are high, which is more than any public tennis, MLB or table-tennis repo surveyed here can claim. If the three deciding experiments — CLV against Pinnacle, hold-to-settlement maker P&L by VPIN, and the decorrelation beta — all fail, the remaining defensible position is to keep the paper harness running as a venue-efficiency monitor and stop trading, which the evidence says is where most participants should already be.
